# Product Hunt Listing — again

## Name

**again — remember what your household actually liked**

## Tagline (55 chars)

## Description

Every recipe online is 4.7 stars, because only people who already wanted to make a dish bother to rate it. again throws ratings out. After you cook, it asks one question — "Would you make it again?" — plus the occasional head-to-head: "Which would you rather make again?" Forced choice builds your household's real ranked rotation, not the internet's.

The Tonight screen gives you 3 picks with plain reasons: "Everyone liked it · takes 25 min · not made in 6 weeks." Logging a repeat meal is 3 taps.

Free, no signup, and your data stays on your device. It's a PWA — add it to your home screen. Try it with demo data in about 10 seconds.

## First maker comment

Hi Product Hunt — solo maker here.

This started with a small observation that wouldn't leave me alone: every recipe on the internet is rated 4.7 stars. Look one up right now — it's 4.7. That's not because every recipe is great. It's because the only people who rate a recipe are the ones who already wanted to make it, made it, and felt fine about it. The signal is broken at the source.

Meanwhile, my household kept having the same 7pm argument: "what should we make tonight?" We'd cooked hundreds of dinners together. Some were clearly winners. Nobody wrote any of it down, so we kept re-deciding from scratch.

So I removed ratings entirely. There isn't a star anywhere in again. Instead, after you cook, you get one question: "Would you make it again?" Yes or no. Sometimes you get a matchup between two dishes you've both said yes to: "Which would you rather make again?" That's it. Forced choice is a much more honest signal than a 1–5 scale, and over a few weeks it produces something no recipe site has: a ranked list of what *your* household actually wants to eat.

The Tonight screen turns that into 3 suggestions with reasons you can check — "Everyone liked it · takes 25 min · not made in 6 weeks." No black box, no "our algorithm thinks."

Honest fine print, since I'd rather you hear it from me:

- It's a beta, built by one person.
- Your data lives on your device only. Privacy upside, but no sync yet — a household invite sets up the shared name on another device, but logs don't sync between devices. That's the top of my roadmap.
- It's a PWA, not an App Store app. Install means "add to home screen."
- No AI, no accounts, no email required, free.

The "Explore with demo data" button shows the whole thing in about 10 seconds without you entering anything. I'd genuinely like to hear where the mechanic breaks for your household — that feedback shapes what I build next.
