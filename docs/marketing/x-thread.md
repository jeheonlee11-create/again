**Tweet 1**

Every recipe on the internet is 4.7 stars. The lasagna: 4.7. The tacos: 4.7. The 40-minute "weeknight" risotto: 4.7. Only people who already wanted to cook a dish bother to rate it — so ratings can't tell you what your household will actually eat twice.

**Tweet 2**

So I built again around one question, asked after you cook: "Would you make it again?" Not 1–5 stars. Not a review. A yes or no from the people who actually ate the thing. One honest answer beats 10,000 strangers' stars.

**Tweet 3**

Sometimes it asks a head-to-head instead: "Which would you rather make again — the chili or the dan dan noodles?" Forced choice fixes the signal. Over a few weeks you end up with your household's real ranked dinner rotation.

**Tweet 4**

Then the Tonight screen settles the nightly argument. Three picks, and it shows its work: "Everyone liked it · takes 25 min · not made in 6 weeks." No black box — you can see why each one is there. Logging a repeat meal is 3 taps.

**Tweet 5**

Your food log lives on your device. Not on my server — there isn't one for your data. Honest beta notes: no cross-device sync yet, and it's a web app you add to your home screen, not an App Store app. Built by one person (me).
